# Karaoke at the café

**Maple Harbor first · Puerto Colibrí in both learner languages · 26 November 2026**

Walk into Sunny Cup, turn right at the door and take the microphone. Pick *The Long Way Home*. At 6.27 seconds the singer begins “Every evening, every evening,” and a brass dot lands on each word. In Echo, the café goes quiet after the line. You can sing it back, say it, or just read it. The people at the next table clap at the end whichever way you played.

This is the owner's **“if budget remains”** feature. The first release worth playing is one café, a cover picker, the vocal master, timed lyrics, meanings, Echo and applause. All work is game-side. Cost in services and assets is $0. The extra practice modes and private microphone have separate cut points below.

**Lyric data.** Index fields give ID, collection/order, target and translated titles (`t/tl`), topic, genre, duration and recording. In each song's `L[]`, `s/e` are absolute sung seconds, `sec/lab` identify the section, `src` is the sung line and `lt` its natural translation into the learner's language. `wt[]` supplies the displayed words with punctuation, `w[]` their absolute start/end pairs. A unit in `u[]` supplies text, a gloss and the number of words it spans. MH also has per-unit American IPA in `p`. SME's optional fourth unit field is an inherited key for card identity. Use field 2 (index 1) for the displayed gloss.

For SB songs, `en.s/e/w` times the English singing of `lt` before the Spanish line. At **5.29 s** in SB01 the English “Hello, how are you?” begins. At **8.09 s** Spanish begins with “Hola, ¿cómo estás?”. Keep both rows, the helper row's own word clock and one target-line count. MH G01's opening line ends at **9.15 s**. These are good fixtures for checking early display versus actual sound.

**What the course player calls practice.** Read `src/lib/songs/{types,data,player-engine,segment-audio}.ts`, `src/components/songs/{SongPlayerDom,SongGuide}.tsx` and the song routes in the courses. The vocal plays under a word sweep. “Kartok po manęs” / “Repeat after me” (K) pauses after a line. Its gap is the sung duration × 1.15 ÷ playback rate + 0.8 s, clamped to 2.2–10 s. The player offers repeat/loop, previous/next line, hide glosses or reveal one, sentence translation, notes, text size, listening and comprehension questions. Its speeds are **1, 0.9, 0.8, 0.7, 0.6×**, pitch-preserved on its media element. These are live controls over the same take. There is no separate singer-off switch or scored karaoke performance in these players. The game's road `karaoke.ts` follows short spoken phrases for Word Gates. Course song timing belongs to the radio.

**Where the token goes.** `docs/RADIO.md`, `src/radio/{songs,index,radio,output,lyrics,extras}.ts` and `src/ui/{Radio.tsx,radio.css}` are the game contract. The public `VITE_RADIO_INDEX` needs no token. `filesToken()` can authorize the active course's song JSON, notes and recording under `VITE_RADIO_FILES`. For relative song paths the radio tries the files folder, then the same path on the course site unsigned (including free examples). A recognized absolute recording root is rewritten to the active files prefix and signed there. Other absolute recording URLs get no token. A local index without a files root can use same-site paths. An absent index disables the booth.

## 3. Reading, playing and handing sound back

**Follow the singer.** The lyric card has one generous current line, a quieter upcoming line and an optional meaning below. Use `readSongLines → withSungTranslations → buildTimeline → lineAt` and the shared output's position in **media seconds**. For each `wt` token, use `w` and the radio's 50 ms lead for onset. A brass or coral dot makes one 3 px hop. Weight and an underline mark the word while it is sounding. Respect each word's end, intro, instrumental breaks and outro. Leave the lyric card clear during long breaks and bring the next row in on the radio timeline. A partial or invalid word alignment falls back to readable whole-line timing. Preserve a valid timed `src` even when the radio parser would drop the row for missing word arrays. Validate word spans before offering a bounce or a blank. Missing line timings make practice unavailable for that title and lead back to the picker.

Tap the line or its **Show meaning** button to open the phrase glosses under their spans and the natural sentence underneath. The same button hides them. Meanings stay with the current target line and fold on advance. SB helper rows keep their English wording and independent clock. Show no duplicate English gloss there. A stable, keyboard-focusable meaning control serves the whole line. On tight screens the meaning opens on a scrollable card and pauses play while you read. Closing returns focus and the previous play intent, subject to holds. Lyrics use actual language tags. Avoid live announcements on every syllable.

### Optional microphone

**Use it only for one quiet Echo turn, on explicit request.** The regular game wraps browser Web Speech in `src/convo/speech.ts`. It can return candidate **spoken words**. A loudspeaker can feed it the recorded singer. It cannot separate voices, attribute words to the learner over music, or judge pitch, rhythm, pronunciation quality or singing ability. The regular recognizer may use the browser vendor's service. Its `preferLocal` path can fall back to that service when a setting throws or the language/service fails. It cannot meet the booth's privacy promise as shipped.

The mic extension requires a **strict on-device-only** option at recognizer creation and on every retry. Reuse the game's supported-browser guard. Require the exact installed pack (`en-US` in MH, `es-MX` in both PC layers), availability already `available`, and successful `processLocally = true` with readback before any start. No pack installation, remote fallback, recording API, waveform capture, storage, transcript logs, analytics or upload. If that guarantee cannot be verified, ship the complete no-mic experience.

In an Echo gap, **Use microphone for this line** freezes the countdown, silences the café bed, voice and effects, then uses the existing capture lease. Show **Listening on this device…** and **Stop listening**. Compare ephemeral recognition alternatives to the displayed target in word order, preserving repeats and Spanish ñ. Light only matched words with a separate underline and the warm **Heard you!** acknowledgement. Clear recognition data at line change, exit or cancellation. Never grade misses. Silence, denial, error or revocation returns to the held lyric with **Continue**. Stop/abort on blur, navigation or a bounded quiet-turn timeout (10 s). Wait for actual release before any further sound, including applause. A missing end signal leaves playback paused until a safe handoff. A new line always starts mic-off.
